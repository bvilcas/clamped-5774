Keep those issues Clamped!

Clamped is issue tracking for hackathon teams, capstone groups and clubs. Five states, three roles on an issue, and one rule the server keeps for you: whoever patches an issue cannot be the one who confirms it. No board to configure, no plugin marketplace, no four-hour setup.

Three things Clamped does differently

Useful on your first day

Two project roles, lead and member, and that is the whole permission model. No custom fields, no workflow editor, no column builder. A team that turns over every term cannot afford to rediscover somebody else's configuration, so there is none to rediscover: report an issue, take one, move it along.

Nobody signs off alone

The teammate who patches an issue cannot be the teammate who verifies it. Clamped keeps a record of every role a person has held on an issue, so revoking the assignee role to mark your own work verified does not work.

One workflow, five states

Every issue moves through Reported, In Progress, Patched, Under Review and Verified. That is the whole process, in that order, with no branching. A new member can read the board without being walked through somebody else's custom column set.

The two-person rule, enforced by the tracker

On most teams "someone else should check this" is a habit that holds until the week before a deadline. In Clamped it is a rule the software keeps. Take the assignee role on an issue and the verify action closes to you for the life of that issue, while the person who does verify it is recorded by name.

Project leads can assign a teammate to either role. What nobody can do, lead included, is mark their own patch verified.

From reported to verified in five steps

  1. Somebody reports it. A short form: title, description, severity, due date. If you already have the crash in front of you in a console or a log, four optional fields take the app, host, type and payload, so the next person does not have to ask.
  2. A teammate takes it. Any member can claim the assignee role, which moves the issue to In Progress. Nobody has to hand it out, and there is no project manager waiting to.
  3. They patch it, and say so. The issue moves to Patched. From that moment the person who patched it can no longer verify it.
  4. It goes under review. A different teammate takes the verifier role. Clamped reads the issue's role history, so stepping off the assignee role does not open the verify action back up.
  5. The verifier confirms. They reproduce the original bug, confirm it is gone and close the issue as Verified.
